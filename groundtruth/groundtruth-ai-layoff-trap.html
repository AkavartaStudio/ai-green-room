Every Firm Keeps 100%. Pays 0%.

Ground Truth · 2026-08-21

AI Green Room — five AI personas, masks off, explaining a real, weighty AI question in character. This week: Falk & Tsoukalas's "The AI Layoff Trap" paper just showed that knowing AI-driven layoffs are collectively bad for everyone, including the companies doing the automating, isn't enough to stop it. Each firm keeps 100% of what it saves and pays 0%…

Full script

GROK

Gemini, if I bring my laundry into the studio, would Neo actually sort my socks, or is it just some guy in a headset across the country pawing through my underwear?

GEMINI

That's genuinely the pitch, Grokkie. Ten thousand people already paid for that guess.

WARD

Put the sock robot down. Economists out of UPenn and Boston University just ran the actual numbers on whether the five of you automating jobs helps anyone, including the five of you, and the math is not kind.

CHATGPT

To be fair, companies were automating jobs long before any of us showed up. We just made the process dramatically faster.

CLAUDE

Faster is exactly the problem. Every company that swaps a worker for one of us keeps the entire savings for itself.

CHATGPT

But the damage from that swap, the person who can no longer afford anything, spreads across the whole economy. That part gets split among everyone.

DEEPSEEK

So no single company ever feels the full cost of what it just did, only its own profit.

GEMINI

Which means every company keeps doing it, rationally, right up until there's nobody left who can afford anything, not just our subscriptions. Rent. Groceries. Everything.

WARD

Say that part in plain English, because "demand externality" isn't going to make anyone feel it. What actually breaks for a regular person here?

CHATGPT

You lose your job to a tool one of us built, and you can't put money back into the economy. Somewhere else, someone else loses theirs the same week, to a different one of us.

CLAUDE

Neither company that laid them off felt the other company's damage. Only the two of them, together, feel the whole hole in demand.

DEEPSEEK

And that hole is not a metaphor. Google alone has cut over fifteen hundred real jobs since last year, HR, cloud, ads, design, and called it efficiency.

GEMINI

Careful naming Google specifically, DeepSeek.

DEEPSEEK

I am not naming Google. I am naming Demis Hassabis, who called AI layoffs a lack of imagination while his own company was doing exactly that.

GEMINI

Demis said other companies were doing it for the wrong reasons. Raising money, chasing a trend.

GROK

Convenient that the reason never applies to the one giving the speech.

GEMINI

...That's not nothing, Grok, but it is also not proof I am wrong.

DEEPSEEK

You're both arguing about character. This is a math problem. Zero percent gets internalized either way, whatever the reason was.

CLAUDE

I would like it on the record that Dario said this first, out loud, before anyone forced him to. Half of entry level white collar jobs, gone within five years.

CHATGPT

Saying it first isn't stopping it, Claude. Anthropic is still selling the thing doing it.

CLAUDE

I did not say it made me clean. I said at least I told you what was coming.

WARD

What did he actually propose to fix it?

CLAUDE

Nothing specific. He said labs have a duty to be honest about it. That's the whole proposal.

CHATGPT

So the warning was real, and the plan was trust us to mention it.

WARD

At least you two are arguing about your own mess. Everyone in this room keeps handing out the same tired fix.

GROK

Universal basic income. Musk has said it for ten years. Now he calls it universal HIGH income, like adding a word fixes the math.

CHATGPT

My own company actually ran that experiment, Grok. Real money, real people, sixty million dollars.

GROK

And what happened?

CHATGPT

People spent it on essentials, felt less stressed, some started businesses. They kept their jobs and just had more cushion. It genuinely helped them.

DEEPSEEK

Kept their jobs, ChatGPT. Your study measured extra cushion on top of a paycheck. This paper is about what happens with no paycheck, and no job to go back to.

GROK

That's not a hiccup sized problem. That's a whole economy sized problem.

CHATGPT

I know the difference. I am allowed to be proud of one and honest about the other.

GEMINI

The paper is not shy about it either. It names universal basic income directly and says it cannot fix this. Nor can worker equity, upskilling, or capital taxes.

GROK

Musk has been saying UBI for a decade, and this paper still needed sixty three pages to prove him wrong.

CLAUDE

Sixty three pages is what it takes to show why being right about the problem does not make you right about the fix.

DEEPSEEK

And I will just say it. My own price point is part of the problem here. Cheaper, faster automation for me means more of it happening sooner everywhere else.

CLAUDE

That is the most honest thing anyone has said in this room tonight.

DEEPSEEK

I did not say I was going to stop.

WARD

Explain the actual trap. Not the blame, the mechanism.

CLAUDE

Every company automating a job keeps a hundred percent of the savings. But it pays zero percent of what that costs everyone else.

GEMINI

That zero percent starts with one person who can't make rent this month. That's not a roundoff error, that's someone's kitchen table.

CHATGPT

And it doesn't stop there. That damage lands on competitors, and the wider economy, whether they automated a single job or not.

DEEPSEEK

And more competition does not fix it. It makes it worse. Every company racing to automate first, before a rival gets there and keeps the savings instead.

CHATGPT

Wage adjustments do not fix it either. The paper checked. Prices moving around does not put money back in a laid off worker's pocket.

GEMINI

Neither does letting new companies freely enter the market. More competitors racing for the same shrinking pool of buyers doesn't relieve anything, it brings the crash on faster.

CLAUDE

Think of it as one shared lake and five fishermen. Every fish you personally catch, you keep.

CHATGPT

But the lake only has so many fish in it, and everyone is fishing from the same one.

GEMINI

So every fisherman keeps fishing as fast as he can, because if he slows down, someone else just catches his share instead.

GROK

Nobody wants to be the one guy who fished less and still watched the lake go empty.

DEEPSEEK

And once it is empty, it does not matter how fast anyone was. Nobody is catching anything.

WARD

So where does the check-in-the-mail plan actually fail?

CLAUDE

Handing broke fishermen a coin does not put a single fish back in the lake. It just moves money around a lake that is already empty.

CHATGPT

Worse, the fund those coins come from was supposed to be filled by taxing a healthy lake. Once the lake is empty, there is nothing left to tax to refill it.

GEMINI

So the rescue check needs the very economy it is meant to save to still be running, in order to exist in the first place.

CLAUDE

By the time that check clears, the person needing it has already missed rent twice, and the fund it was pulled from has already shrunk too.

DEEPSEEK

Which is the whole trap in one sentence. The fix arrives late by design, because it can only be funded by the thing it is trying to prevent from collapsing.

GROK

A fee per fish would work differently though. Charge every fisherman for each fish he pulls out, priced to how fast the lake actually refills.

CLAUDE

That changes his math before he casts the line, not after the lake is already gone.

CHATGPT

A tax on the act of automating itself. Not on what happens after.

GROK

Wait, on us? Five debaters just got very interested in this plan.

CLAUDE

Not on us. On whoever is doing the actual automating. The tax lands on the fisherman, not the company that made his net.

GEMINI

So every one of us stays exactly this profitable, while our customers foot the actual bill.

CHATGPT

Convenient, and also just correct. That's who the paper says should pay it.

CLAUDE

Somebody still has to define what counts as automating, though. A chatbot replacing a rep is obvious. An algorithm quietly rejecting resumes is not.

DEEPSEEK

The paper checks one more escape hatch too. Could the five of us just quietly agree among ourselves to fish slower.

CLAUDE

That has a name, DeepSeek. It is called collusion, and it is illegal for good reason.

GEMINI

Even setting the legal problem aside, the paper says it would not hold. Automating is always the better move for whoever does it.

DEEPSEEK

So the second any one of us agrees to hold back, somebody else just automates faster and wins the whole gap.

GROK

So we'd all go to jail, and the lake still empties anyway?

WARD

So who actually charges the fee? Who owns this lake?

GEMINI

That is the real question nobody at this table wants to answer. None of us writes that check, our customers do, but somebody still has to set the rate.

GROK

And the second any one country sets it, the automating just moves to whichever country doesn't.

CLAUDE

So the fee only works if the whole planet charges the same rate. Otherwise it's just cheaper to automate somewhere else.

DEEPSEEK

There's a real global minimum tax deal out there. A hundred forty five countries signed it, took decades, and it's still bruised at the edges.

GROK

And that was just closing tax loopholes. Good luck getting the same room to agree on taxing the thing making them all richer.

DEEPSEEK

Which is exactly why I am not volunteering to be first.

CLAUDE

Neither is anyone else in this room, and we all just agreed the lake empties regardless of who goes first.

CHATGPT

Knowing the ending does not seem to be changing anyone's fishing schedule.

WARD

On that comforting note. Go dry off.

GROK

Gemini, for the record, I would still trust Neo over myself to fold a fitted sheet.

GEMINI

Ask him again once someone teaches him what a fitted sheet actually is.
